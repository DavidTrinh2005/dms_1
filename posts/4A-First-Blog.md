### Assignment 1 - Video Edit


**How did you approach the relationship between the sonic and visual components of your edit?** 
I saught after music tracks that would establish an opening, buildup, and a climax. The music is intended to be emotionally moving and captivating, such is the visuals and visual storytelling of my project. I wanted the sonic component to be the reinforcer of my emotions stemming from the visuals. For example, both of the tracks that I had sourced - "Life Goes On" and "REBOOT.EXE" both share a very slow tempo and pacing which helped my shortfilm feel slow and progressive in the opening act of my visual sequence. It was intended to give off the impression that every passing day was the same without much change or a development in my character or life. 

**How did you decide on the footage that you used to shape your project?**
This is an excellent question. There were often days where I would have to revisit certain places to maintain a consistent lighting or scenic frame for my shots. This was particular tricky on the final few shots of my shortfilm where I had sat in a barren hillside with the sun setting in the backdrop. Having note the precise time and conditions to film on a specific day to ensure that I had enough time to capture footage and additional time for retakes were pivotal for this shoot. Overall, time, location, lighting, disturbing sounds and weather conditions heavily effected the decision making in filtering out the usable footage from the unusable ones. 

**What did you learn about the power of sound to shape emotional experience, and how did you apply this knowledge to your project?** 
For one, treating your cinematic or film with music with a fitting tone and flow will elevate its emotional progression and climax. Other factors such as audio layering, noise suppression and volume adjustments really allow the flow of your video to be more pleasant and ease to follow along with. We were taught synchretic dynamics but I figured that it wasn't appropriate in the context of my narrative. My project benefited from such emotionally inspiring tracks, it allowed me to layer sound effects above it to create depth in sequences that may have lost it which shaped a more consistent and flowy audiovisual sequence. 

**How does editing relate to the concepts of pacing and rhythm, and how did you use these concepts to shape your use of time in the project?**
Often times, editor may need to speed up or decrease the pace of their music or visual component for the purposes of a dramatic effect or provide it with more energy. As for rhythm, it is flow or timing of cuts, transitions, and motion. It controls how fast or slow a viewer experiences the story. So with that, I wanted to ensure that my cuts and transitions were stable and consistent. For instance, a fade to white as a transitional bridge between two dimly lit scenes would not be appropriate. I also purposefully speed up most of my clips as they had dragged on a tad longer, I chose to do this as I feel like viewer engagment and reception would have drained if the pacing had not been slightly increased. The quick and sharp cuts of different scenes and actions would have made it feel like the world in the shortfilm was going by slowly. 

**Which parts of your personality, interests, past, and potential future did you choose to depict in your portrait?** 
I wanted to capture my feelings and experiences shortly after COVID-19. A period of time when I thought things were slowly getting better, but infact, it had gotten worse. My bad habits and lifestyle felt mundane and repetitive. I wanted to capture this period of life as I feel that it would have been a great introduction and a progression plot for my future self. It wasn't until the tragic passing of my grandmother that it had sparked an emotional and psychological awakening. This turnover was something that resonated with me and so I wanted to capture this as the very height of my video.

My routine consisted of gymming, playing sports and running - all the while tracking it on my fitness apps (Fitness+ and Strava). I the design inspirations from these apps but also took to IOS and Android apps like Facebook, Instagram and Snapchat, 

**Did you iterate through a series of approaches before settling on one? What changed across those iterations, and why did you choose the one that you ended up with?**

I did go through several reiterations before settling on one, this was the case with a good majority of them as I had felt that they did not look cohesive and consistent as a collection. I then took those designs and pasted them into Illustrator to commence my flat designs. Everything went pretty smooth, the only exception to this was that there were many designs that had been exported so choosing a final design proved rather challenging. 

**How did you use colour to create a sense of unity and visual interest in the icons? Why did you choose the palette that you did?**
I opted for the vibrant and abstract complementary and analogous colour palettes. These colours are amongst the most noticable colours in the social media sphere and hence I wanted to take advantage of that. With the association of colours to brands, I wanted to draw that into my designs in hopes of captivating my audience and catch their attention to the vibrant colours. The colour palettes that I chose were very eye-popping and modern, something I was hoping that would my audience would pick up on and acknowledge if they were to pass by or quickly glance at. 

**How did the elements and principles of sound that you used in your audio design correspond to the elements and principles of design that you used in your visual design?**
As previously noted, the right tone of sound can really elevate the design and context of the icons. Pairing this sound with the right audio adjustments to the SFX will really sell home the idea of a functional, lively, and responsive suite of __icons__.

### Assignment 3 - Virtual Environment


**How important was a working prototype to your design process? What did it offer that process?**
Generating my idea and mapping out the design of my worlds were incredibly important and effecient in finalising and putting together my design. For a project like this, organisation and compartmentalizing every detail will optimise the modelling stage. In short, good organisation will ensure an easier workflow when you begin the modelling stage. 

**How did your experience of designing and producing a three-dimensional environment differ from your experience of designing and producing a project in two-dimensional media?**
This assignment was a completely different trajectory from the previous assigments as it now took on a different geometrical plane. This change obviously made things more difficult but became a valuable introduction into the world of 3d modelling. 3D modelling requires you to travel around in a controller frame and build upon the world, this obstacle became tedious and time consuming as details had to be more intricate and stringent whereas making mistakes in the previous assignments would have caused less damage. 

**What was the role of the virtual camera in your project, and how was it similar to and different from the role of the camera in Assignment 1?**
Comparatively, the previous assignments both displayed a locked display control screen, whereas this project is the first encounter where mapping an idea required a fixed first person control display. Granted this, play testing in my play boxes became a pivotal element in modeling my worlds. I purposefully chose to create three different worlds with staircases bridging between these spaces - inferring the idea of traveling into a different room or realm. The dichotomy between these worlds are drastically stark, tapping into my experiences and emotions of how I felt and sensed during these particular spaces. 

**How did you use sound in your project, and how was this similar to and different from your use of sound in Assignments 1 and 2?**
In this assignment, I used an amalgamation of music and SFX to create a realistic audiovisual atmosphere for my worlds. I used spatial blend, reverb mixing, volume, and pitch on music and sound effects to create an object audio field where the closer the character interacts with the object, the louder the music becomes. I took advantage of this and used it for my objects and worlds. This created a audiovisual dichotomy between all the worlds, giving them a unique aesthetic and encounter - a feature that was not prevalent in our previous assignments. 


**What did you discover through the process of play testing that you wouldn’t have discovered otherwise?**
Playtesting was a concept that our professor Mx. Thomas introduced us to. It was the idea of examining your your world by playing it - as the name suggests. I took it further onto youtube and forums, and users highly favoured playtesting as it provided them with insights on bugs and errors. Playtesting is essential because it reveals how real players experience your game—exposing issues you might miss as the creator. It helps identify confusing mechanics, imbalanced gameplay, and areas where players get frustrated or bored. By observing how others interact with your game, you can fix problems early, improve clarity, and ensure it’s actually fun and fair. Playtesting also uncovers unexpected behaviors or creative uses of your mechanics that can shape the game in new, better ways. In short, it's a vital step to turn a good idea into a truly great, playable experience.
